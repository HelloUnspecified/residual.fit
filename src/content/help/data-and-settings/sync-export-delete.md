## Where your data lives

Everything you plan and log is stored on your iPhone. With iCloud on, it's also kept in your private iCloud database, which keeps your devices in sync. There's no account: nothing to sign up for or log in to.

Two things do leave the phone when you use them. Food search sends what you type to USDA FoodData Central. Planning with Claude sends Anthropic what a plan needs, and only once you've said yes; what it sends is in [Choose who plans](/help/planning/who-plans/). The [privacy policy](/privacy/) has the full detail.

## Check iCloud sync

Open `More` and look under Account and data. The **iCloud sync** row says **On** or **Off**. It's a status, not a switch. The line under the section says why:

- “Your days and foods sync through your own iCloud account.” Sync is on.
- “iCloud is off on this device, so your data stays here. Sign in to iCloud to sync.” Sign in to iCloud in Settings and the row turns on by itself.
- “iCloud sync is unavailable right now, so your data is stored only on this device.” Residual couldn't open its iCloud store this time and is keeping everything on the iPhone so the app still works. Quit and reopen Residual to try again.

If a change can't be saved, a message names it and says “Couldn't save … right now — it'll retry automatically.” You don't need to do anything.

## Sync doesn't show on another device

1. Sign both devices in to the same Apple Account.
2. On each, open Settings, tap your name, then iCloud, and check iCloud is on for Residual.
3. Open `More` on each and check that **iCloud sync** reads **On**.

Changes catch up when both devices have a connection. Offline, each one keeps working on its own copy.

## Export to a spreadsheet

1. Open `More → Export to a spreadsheet`.
2. Under Days, pick **7 days**, **30 days**, **All**, or **Pick** to choose a From and To date.
3. Leave **Include planned meals** on to get each meal's plan beside what you ate, or turn it off for only the meals you logged.
4. Tap **Export CSV** and choose where it goes: Files, Mail, Numbers or anywhere else.

The file is named for its days, such as “Residual 2026-11-01 to 2026-11-07.csv”. It has one row per food, and one for each day with nothing tracked: the day, the meal, what you ate and what was planned (grams, protein, carbs, fat and calories), where the numbers came from, and that day's targets. **Columns** on the same screen lists every column. It opens in Numbers, Excel or Google Sheets, and you can hand it to a coach, a dietitian or an AI assistant.

> [!NOTE] When the app is locked
>
> If your subscription or trial has ended, More isn't reachable. Tap the “…” button at the top of the Residual Plus screen and choose **Export my data**. It's the same export. See [Locked out, or asked to update](/help/troubleshooting/locked-or-update-required/).

## Delete your data

- Delete single meals, foods and notes in the app, where you see them.
- Deleting Residual from your iPhone removes its data from the phone.
- To remove the iCloud copy, open Settings, tap your name, then iCloud, then Manage Storage, then Residual.
- To delete anything we hold, such as a support email or your newsletter address, write to support@residual.fit.

The plan requests Residual keeps on your iPhone for planning have their own delete button: **Delete recent plan requests** in `More → AI planning`.

## Bring data in

Residual doesn't import history from other apps or from Apple Health. Starting fresh takes a few minutes: set your targets, plan a day, and your saved foods and meals build up as you go.
